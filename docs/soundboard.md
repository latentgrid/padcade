# Soundboard

## Make your first loop

1. Open Soundboard. Hold an empty track arrow on the right. Count four high ticks, then play when the arrow blinks. You have eight beats for your part.
2. On Drums, use the purple row, third from the bottom. Play **Electric kick** on beats 1 and 3 and **Electric snare** on 2 and 4. When the part loops, hold the same arrow and add the **Electric closed hat** on each beat. These are the first three pads from the left.
3. Hold another arrow to record a different part. Tap a recorded arrow to mute or unmute it during playback. To remove a layer, hold **Undo** and tap that track’s arrow. Earlier layers stay in the song.
4. Press **Stop Clip** to stop or start the loop. To capture a whole performance, tap the round **Record** button on the left.

Up to 24 sounds play at once, the song’s and yours together. When more start, the quietest one gives way.

A new track plays back as you played it. Use **Quantise** to tidy its timing afterwards.

When you open another page, the loop waits and starts again from its first beat when you come back to the Soundboard. While you are recording a layer or a performance it keeps running, so that sounds from the other page are recorded too.

### Find a drum

The bottom three rows are playable kits. On each one, the first five pads are **kick, snare, closed hat, open hat and clap**. Learn a beat on one row, then move your fingers up a row to try a different sound.

| Row from bottom | Sounds |
|---|---|
| 1 | Main kit |
| 2 | Original Jam kit, including Classic kick and Jam clap |
| 3 | Electric kit |
| 4 | Alternate kicks on the left, pitched Electric kicks (C1, G1, C2, G2) on the right |
| 5 | Snares, fills and accents |
| 6 | Toms |
| 7 | Hand percussion and blocks |
| 8 | Hats, cymbals and other metal percussion |

**Jam classics** recreates the original 28-pad instrument with plucks near the top, bass near the bottom and drums along the bottom edge. In **Memes**, the third row from the top holds earlier sound variants. The clip library also includes the earlier bass and pluck voices for custom pads.

### Play Synth

On **Synth**, the bottom two rows play Studio bass, the next two play Jam bass, and the next two play Jam pluck. Each pair works like a piano. Play the natural notes on the lower row and the sharps on the pads above them. The bright C pads help you find your place, and the unlit gaps sit where a piano has no black key. Choose **Synth high** in Shift to continue into the upper register.

### Play chords with one finger

**All chords** has four piano-shaped pairs of rows. From the bottom up they play major, minor, upper major and upper minor chords. Move four rows straight up to play the same chord one octave higher. For example, the pad above D plays D♯ major in the first pair and D♯ minor in the second. D♯ is the same pitch as E♭. Hold **Sends** to sustain a chord after lifting your finger.

## Play over it

For an even playing level, open **Soundboard → Edit layout → Settings** in the browser and turn off **Pressure sensitivity**. Soft and hard taps then play at the same volume. Turn it back on to use each pad’s saved strike response. Existing recordings keep their dynamics.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Shift** | Choose a sound bank. All banks feed the same song. The upper-right tempo pads change speed by one BPM per tap. Hold to keep changing it. |
| **Track arrows** | Empty arrows are dark. Recorded tracks are lit when audible and dim when muted. Tap a filled track to mute or unmute it; a tap on an empty one does nothing. Hold either to record after the count-in. A filled track keeps its earlier layers, and a muted one is heard while you record on it. Tap that arrow during the count-in to cancel or during recording to finish the layer early. |
| **Click** | Toggle the metronome, even while stopped. |
| **Quantise** | Hold it to see which tracks contain notes, each as bright as its timing. Tap a lit arrow to step that track’s timing. Quantise by itself does nothing. There are four settings, brighter with each: your own timing, **Gentle** (hits nudged halfway to the nearest sixteenth note), **Fine** (snapped to the nearest thirty-second, which keeps quick double hits apart) and **Tight** (snapped to the nearest sixteenth). |
| **Undo** | Hold it and tap a recorded track to remove its newest layer. Undo by itself never removes a layer: it cancels a pending deletion or a queued song, or brings back what was deleted last. |
| **Sends** | Hold for sustain on piano, soft keys, warm pads and chords. In the simulator, Shift-click to hold, then click to release. |
| **Track Select** | Opens the 16 song slots; tap it again to go back. An empty slot starts a new song immediately. During playback, a saved song starts at the next loop. |

## Capture a full performance

The round **Record** button on the left glows red when a performance can be recorded. Tap it to capture your live playing and backing loop together. It pulses red until you tap again to save.

Hold **Record** to open **Takes**. **Record Arm** and **Mute** are free for a custom assignment in Edit layout. Tap a lit pad to play its take immediately. Tap it again to stop. To remove a take, press **Delete**, choose its pad, then press **Delete** again.

In the browser, each take in **Takes** can be played, renamed by clicking its name, downloaded as a stereo WAV or as an editable project, and deleted. The song’s own menu, the three dots beside **Songs**, holds **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Export song** for the eight-beat loop, **Import song** and **Delete song**. An imported song goes into the first empty slot, together with its sounds.

An exported song or project is a ZIP for going on in a music program on a computer. It holds one sound file per track with the tempo in its name, the notes as a MIDI file, every sound used, and for each track an instrument in SFZ format that gives those notes the board’s own sounds in any SFZ player. Chord pads are the exception: their notes are in the MIDI file for an instrument of your choice, and their own sound is only in the track’s sound file. Muted song tracks keep their audio for editing. Their WAV filenames say “muted”, and `README.txt` lists which tracks to mute in your music program to hear the saved mix. A song can bring up to 256 clips, each up to twenty seconds long, with 60 MiB of samples in total. Export tells you if a song exceeds the board’s import limits. You can still use its files on your computer.

On a track in the browser, tap the row to mute it, press the red dot to record on it, and drag the grip at its start to move it; the arrow keys move it too. What you clear or delete there shows a line with **Undo** for a few seconds; after that, **Bring back** in the song’s menu, or under the takes, still returns it. For your own sounds, choose **Edit layout** and use **Upload clips**, **Record a clip** or [import a sound bank](sound-banks.md). Click a pad there and **Upload** or **Record** puts the new clip straight onto that pad. An upload never replaces a clip you have: under a name that is taken it becomes a clip of its own, with a number after the name. A clip of your own has a bin beside its ▶ in the clip library: it removes the clip for good, from every pad. **Clear** on a pad only takes the clip off that pad.

**Record a clip** records with the microphone of the phone or computer you are on, up to twenty seconds. Afterwards a picture of the recording shows the part that will be kept, between two handles. It starts out without the silence before and after; drag the handles to cut it as you like. The round button beside the picture plays the cut and pauses it. A red mark shows where the playback is and stays where you pause, so that you can see where to cut; a handle dragged close to it lands exactly on it. A click on the picture puts the mark there, and listening goes on from it. Then name the clip and save it. A browser only lends its microphone to a page opened securely, so from another device the button first explains this and then opens the same page again on the board’s secure address, with the recorder in it. On the way the browser warns once that it cannot verify the board: it made its own certificate, because nobody issues one for a device in your home. Choose to continue. In the simulator, and whenever you already opened the board by its `https://` address, the recorder opens at once.
